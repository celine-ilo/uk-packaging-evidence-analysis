## Figure 1 — Recovery route by material, 2023–2025

::blue:: SHOWS. A 100% stacked bar per material, splitting three years of evidenced tonnage between UK reprocessing and export.

::blue:: SOURCE. `quarterly_table1_recycling_clean.csv`, 459 rows, twelve quarterly files. Tableau sheet `01 Recovery route by material 2023-2025`. Filtered to the two measures `Waste accepted for UK reprocessing` and `Waste exported for overseas reprocessing`; the other three measures in that column are excluded because they double count.

::teal:: NUMBERS. Wood 99.7% UK, glass other 99.5%, glass re-melt 81.6%, plastic 49.1%, aluminium 32.1%, paper and board 29.6%, steel 28.7%. Three-year totals: UK 12,063,954 tonnes, export 12,089,202, combined 24,153,156. Overall export share 50.1%.

::teal:: MEANS. The aggregate half-and-half split conceals complete divergence between materials. Steel, paper and aluminium are discharged overwhelmingly abroad; wood and glass other almost entirely at home. Since a PRN and a PERN discharge an obligation identically, nothing in the regime produces this pattern deliberately. It is what relative price produces when two routes are made fungible.

::rust:: DOES NOT SUPPORT. Any statement about change over time: twelve quarters are aggregated into one bar and the figure is silent on trend. Nor does it support a claim about physical recycling outcomes, since it measures tonnage accepted or exported, not tonnage verified as recycled to standard at destination.

::rust:: Paper Composting and Wood Composting appear with no bar. Both carry zero in all 51 published values across the three years — the accreditation categories exist and are unused. For a presentation version, filter them out and state this in the caption rather than leaving an unexplained gap.
