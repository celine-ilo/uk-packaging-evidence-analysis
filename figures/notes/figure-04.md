## Figure 4 — Packaging content by protocol, 2025

::blue:: SHOWS. For each material and each agreed protocol, the share of received tonnage that counts as packaging. A protocol is the agreement that fixes what proportion of a load is treated as packaging for evidence purposes.

::blue:: SOURCE. `quarterly_table2_protocols_clean.csv`, 2,058 rows. Tableau sheet `04 Packaging content by protocol 2025`, filtered to 2025. Uses a calculated field, `Packaging content`, dividing net received by gross received.

::teal:: NUMBERS. Steel `1+2 Mixed` converts 241,047 tonnes gross into 1,326 tonnes net — 0.6%. Steel `2` is 1.1%, `Other (agreed or sampled)` 1.3%, `Fragmentised` 4.7%. Across all protocols steel ran 1,198,698 tonnes gross in 2025 to yield 69,812 net, 5.8% overall. At the other end, glass re-melt, glass other and wood under `100% Packaging` are exactly 100%, and paper's AAIG EN643 97.5% grade measures 97.4%.

::teal:: MEANS. A credited tonne does not mean a tonne of packaging was recycled. For most of steel's volume it means a load arrived, an agreed fraction of it was deemed to be packaging, and evidence was issued against that fraction. Steel packaging evidence is largely a by-product of general scrap processing rather than a packaging recycling operation.

::teal:: This is the mechanism behind steel's 71.3% export share in Figure 1. The protocols determine how much evidence a given physical flow generates, and they vary by two orders of magnitude between materials.

::teal:: The figure is reliable because the two tables reconcile. Net received, summed across a material's protocols, equals that material's UK reprocessing figure in Table 1 exactly, for all 84 year-quarter-material combinations.

::rust:: DOES NOT SUPPORT. Any claim that the protocols are wrong, fraudulent or improperly applied. They are agreed with the regulator and lawful, and a low packaging fraction in a mixed scrap stream is a reasonable description of reality rather than an abuse of it. The finding is about what the resulting number represents, not about whether anyone is cheating.

::rust:: It also cannot establish whether the agreed fractions are accurate. Verifying that would need sampling data from reprocessor sites, which is not published.

::plum:: OPEN. Two protocols show no bar. Aluminium's `Other (agreed or sampled)` carried 839 tonnes across the three years but nothing in 2025. Paper's `AAIG EN643 grade 1.04.01 (70%)` is zero in all three years — an accreditation category that exists in the schema and is never used, like the composting categories in Figure 1. Both are left in rather than filtered out, so the absence is visible.
