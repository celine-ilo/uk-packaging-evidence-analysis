## Figure 2 — Recovery route over time, 2023–2025

::blue:: SHOWS. The same split as Figure 1, resolved into twelve quarters rather than aggregated, to test whether the balance moves.

::blue:: SOURCE. Same file and filter as Figure 1. Tableau sheet `02 Recovery route over time 2023-2025`.

::teal:: NUMBERS. Export share by quarter: 51.1, 50.0, 48.5, 50.9 (2023); 49.1, 47.8, 48.3, 52.2 (2024); 52.3, 48.8, 49.8, 52.1 (2025). Lowest 47.8%, highest 52.3%, spread 4.5 percentage points. Annual figures 50.1%, 49.3%, 50.7%.

::teal:: MEANS. The balance does not move. Across twelve consecutive quarters it never leaves a 4.5-point band around half, with no trend in either direction. Total evidenced tonnage fell about 5% over the same period, so the stability is in the ratio rather than in the volume.

::teal:: The policy timing is what makes this interesting, and it has to be stated precisely. Data reporting began under the 2023 Data Regulations. The Producer Responsibility Obligations (Packaging and Packaging Waste) Regulations 2024, SI 2024/1332, were made on 11 December 2024; under regulation 1(2) the substantive provisions came into force on 1 January 2025, with a short list in regulation 1(3), including Schedule 7, which establishes the scheme administrator, commencing a day after they were made on 12 December 2024. PackUK's first operational year ran from 1 April 2025, which is a scheme year rather than a commencement date. The first Notices of Liability, calculated on 2024 packaging data, were issued on 14 October 2025, with the first direct debit collection on 3 December 2025. Recyclability modulation, the instrument actually intended to change producer behaviour, applies from assessment year 2026 to 2027, which begins in April 2026, at a factor of 1.2 for Red-rated material.

::teal:: So of the twelve quarters, eleven had no fee in effect at all, the twelfth had a flat unmodulated base fee, and none had the behavioural instrument in effect. This figure is therefore a pre-modulation baseline, not a verdict on whether pEPR works. Its value is that the before-picture is documented on validated data, which makes a 2026 and 2027 comparison possible later.

::rust:: DOES NOT SUPPORT. The claim that policy failed, or that producers ignored it. No instrument in this window priced the choice between domestic reprocessing and export, so there is nothing here for producers to have responded to. The data cannot distinguish a policy that had no effect from a policy that was not aimed at this balance; separating those requires reading the instruments, not the tonnages.

::rust:: It also does not support an argument that change simply takes longer than two years. This balance is set by relative price — reprocessing capacity, export market rates, shipping costs, destination import rules — and those reprice within months. China's 2018 import restrictions moved UK export flows inside a single year. Flatness here indicates that relative price did not change, not that the system responds slowly.

::blue:: VERIFIED 13 November 2026. The commencement dates above were checked against SI 2024/1332 on legislation.gov.uk, regulation 1(2) and (3), and against PackUK's annual report for 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026 for the invoicing dates. The claim that eleven of the twelve quarters had no fee in effect holds. One distinction worth keeping in mind when citing this: the 1.2 modulation factor is set by PackUK's Producer Fee Modulation Policy Statement, issued under regulation 64, and not by the statutory instrument itself, so it can be revised without amending the regulations.
