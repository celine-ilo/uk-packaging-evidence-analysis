## Figure 3 — Evidence timing, 2023–2025

::blue:: SHOWS. A 100% stacked bar per year, splitting evidence issued against each compliance year into the part raised within the year and the part raised in the January after it closed.

::blue:: SOURCE. `quarterly_table1_recycling_clean.csv`. Tableau sheet `03 Evidence timing 2023-2025`. Filtered to `Total PRNs/PERNs issued` and `PRNs/PERNs issued in January following`. The January column exists only in the Q4 file of each year, which is why Quarter is removed from this view.

::teal:: NUMBERS. 2023: 7,393,610 tonnes in-year against 789,139 in January, a 9.6% share. 2024: 7,115,675 against 956,589, 11.9%. 2025: 6,439,515 against 1,089,821, 14.5%. The January share rises every year while the in-year total falls.

::teal:: MEANS. Evidence is increasingly raised after the compliance year has closed. The window grew by half again in two years, and in 2025 more than a million tonnes of evidence was issued in a single month against a year that had already ended.

::teal:: The practical consequence is that summing the four quarterly files understates annual issuance by that amount. Table 4 of the Q4 file carries the true annual total, and quarters plus the January window reconciles to it exactly for all 21 year-material pairs. Anyone building an annual figure from the quarterly series alone will be low by a tenth or more, and the error grows each year.

::teal:: An independent dataset supports the same pattern from a different measure. The NPWD cumulative snapshots record tonnage entered rather than evidence issued, and show 9.9%, 11.6% and 11.3% of each year's final tonnage arriving after the year closed.

::rust:: DOES NOT SUPPORT. Any claim that this is avoidance or gaming. A January issuance window is a legitimate feature of the compliance calendar: material recycled in late December cannot have evidence raised against it until it is processed and reported. The figure establishes that the window is growing; it says nothing about why.

::rust:: It also cannot separate the possible causes — slower reporting, deliberate timing, a shift in when material is processed, or administrative backlog at the regulator. Distinguishing those needs operator-level data that is not published.
