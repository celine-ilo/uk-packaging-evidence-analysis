## Figure 7 — Outstanding returns by quarter, 2023–2025

::blue:: SHOWS. One stacked bar per quarter, counting accredited reprocessors and exporters that had not submitted their quarterly return at the time the summary was published. Split by stream: exporter against reprocessor.

::blue:: SOURCE. `quarterly_table3_outstanding_clean.csv`, 140 rows, from the Outstanding Returns sheet of each quarterly recycling summary. Tableau sheet `07 Outstanding returns by quarter 2023-2025`.

::teal:: NUMBERS. 140 outstanding returns across twelve quarters, from 63 distinct companies. Q4 accounts for 69 of the 140 — 49.3% of all outstanding returns fall in the quarter that is one quarter of the year. The Q4 counts are 17 (2023), 29 (2024) and 23 (2025), against a Q1–Q3 range of 5 to 13.

::teal:: Exporters are 96 of the 140, reprocessors 44. Plastic is 93 of the 140, and plastic exporters alone are 56 — two fifths of every outstanding return in the dataset. Size is evenly split, 70 small and 70 large, so this is not a small-operator problem.

::teal:: 32 of the 63 companies appear more than once. Duclo Recycling Ltd appears in 8 of the 12 quarters and Trugreen Solutions Ltd in 7. Half the companies appear once; the other half are repeat non-filers.

::teal:: MEANS. Every evidenced tonnage in Figures 1 to 6 is a sum over the returns that arrived. The returns in this figure did not arrive, so whatever those operators handled is absent from the totals. The published figures are therefore a floor, not a measurement, and the floor is lowest in Q4 — the quarter in which compliance is determined.

::teal:: The concentration in plastic export is the sharpest signal. It is the route with the highest PRN prices in the window, the least direct oversight, and the most missing returns, and those three facts sit together.

::rust:: DOES NOT SUPPORT. Any claim about how much tonnage is missing. The table names companies, not volumes. 140 returns could be a rounding error or a material gap; nothing published lets us size it.

::rust:: Nor does it support a rate. There is no count of accredited reprocessors and exporters in any of the seven datasets, so 63 companies cannot be turned into a percentage of the accredited population. The denominator is the missing piece, and its absence is itself a finding about what the published data permits.

::rust:: "Outstanding" is a status at the moment of publication, not a permanent failure. A return filed late would still appear here. The data does not say which of the 140 were later submitted, so this is a lateness-and-absence measure combined, and the two cannot be separated.

::plum:: OPEN. The accredited-operator list is published separately by the environment agencies. Obtaining it would turn this count into a rate and make the Q4 pattern far stronger. Worth pursuing before the report is written.

::plum:: Whether the 2026 monthly data shows the same pattern. `monthly_outstanding_clean.csv` counts unsubmitted returns by material and size band: 60 across January to September 2026, of which 48 fall in September alone. That September figure is almost certainly reporting lag rather than non-compliance, and treating it as a spike would be a mistake.
